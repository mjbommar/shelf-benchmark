# Library of Congress Classification: Bias Analysis

## Executive Summary

The Library of Congress Classification (LCC) system has documented Western, American, and Christian biases that stem from its origins as a practical system designed for the Library of Congress's specific collections. However, these limitations do not invalidate SHELF's claim to "universal" coverage for three key reasons:

1. **No superior alternative exists**: All major classification systems (DDC, UDC, CLC) exhibit cultural biases reflecting their origins
2. **SHELF mitigates structural biases**: Synthetic generation enables balanced coverage across all LCC classes, forms, topics, and geographies
3. **LCC remains the most comprehensive**: Despite biases, LCC covers a broader range of human knowledge than any competing system

## Known Biases in LC Classification System

### 1. Religious Bias

**Nature of bias**: Christianity receives disproportionate attention in subclass B (Philosophy, Psychology, Religion)

**Evidence**:
- Multiple subclasses dedicated to The Bible and Christianity
- Judaism (BM), Islam (BP), and Buddhism (BQ) each receive single subclasses
- Non-Western religions and spiritual beliefs are marginalized
- Reflects a "westernized perspective that prioritizes Christianity"

**Source**: [Stonehill College Library Newsletter](https://www.stonehill.edu/library/library-newsletter/news/the-library-of-congress-outdated-biased-classifications/)

### 2. American/European Geographic Bias

**Nature of bias**: Schedules D, E, and F (History) reflect American and European focus

**Evidence**:
- D (World History) emphasizes European history
- E and F (History of the Americas) provide detailed American coverage
- Non-Western regions receive less granular classification
- Later-developed K (Law) shows more global balance

**Source**: [Wikipedia - Library of Congress Classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress_Classification)

### 3. Racial and Ethnic Terminology

**Nature of bias**: Outdated and potentially harmful terminology

**Evidence from African American Studies**:
- Use of terms like "Negro," "miscegenation," "Black Muslims"
- "Colonial orientation" that sees Black people as "passive objects acted on"
- Materials about Native Americans placed under "History" (past-focused)
- Indigenous peoples grouped as "American Indians" despite tribal diversity

**Source**: [Project MUSE - Browsing through Bias](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/706989)

### 4. LGBTQ+ Classification Issues

**Nature of bias**: Harmful categorization and outdated terminology

**Evidence**:
- LGBTQ identities bordered by "Sexual Deviations" and "Sadism"
- Some historical queer books classified under "Sexual Deviations"
- LGBTQ books as subcategory of "Sexual Life" (sex-focused)
- "Man-Woman Relationships" as separate category (relationship-focused)

**Source**: [Stonehill College Library Newsletter](https://www.stonehill.edu/library/library-newsletter/news/the-library-of-congress-outdated-biased-classifications/)

### 5. Gender Bias

**Nature of bias**: Male-default assumptions in subject headings

**Evidence**:
- 4,605 terms identified with "women" vs. 444 with "men"
- Subject headings like "engineers" have "women engineers" but not "male engineers"
- Assumes maleness as default, requiring gendered qualifiers only for women

**Source**: Research by Brendan J. Honick cited in [Stonehill College](https://www.stonehill.edu/library/library-newsletter/news/the-library-of-congress-outdated-biased-classifications/)

### 6. Structural/Epistemological Bias

**Nature of bias**: Western hierarchical thinking embedded in structure

**Evidence**:
- Mutually exclusive categories (at odds with non-Western thinking)
- Hierarchical structure (European intellectual tradition)
- Linear sequence of subcategories (abstract to concrete)
- Interdisciplinary topics difficult to classify

**Source**: [Yes Magazine - Indigenous Library Approach](https://www.yesmagazine.org/social-justice/2019/03/22/decolonize-western-bias-indigenous-library-books)

### 7. Art and Fine Arts Bias

**Nature of bias**: Privileges European fine art over other traditions

**Evidence**:
- Hierarchical structure privileges fine art over craft
- By extension, privileges art by white European men
- Reflects biases of traditional Western art history canon

**Source**: [Panorama - Critiquing the Catalog](https://journalpanorama.org/article/critiquing-the-catalog/)

## Global Adoption Statistics

### United States
- **60% of U.S. academic libraries** use LCC
- **Majority of large research libraries** have adopted LCC
- **Almost all academic law libraries** in the U.S. use LCC
- Public libraries and small academic libraries prefer DDC

**Source**: [LIS Academy - Global Popularity](https://lis.academy/organising-and-managing-information/library-classification-systems-global-popularity/)

### International
- **Limited international footprint** outside North America
- Adopted primarily by:
  - Libraries with strong American influences
  - Research libraries with extensive English-language collections
  - Libraries focusing on American studies
  - Libraries seeking compatibility with LC cataloging

**Source**: [LIS Academy - Global Popularity](https://lis.academy/organising-and-managing-information/library-classification-systems-global-popularity/)

### Factors Limiting Global Adoption
1. No simplified/abridged versions (unlike DDC)
2. Less institutional promotion globally vs. DDC
3. American, European, and Christian bias in design
4. Classification adoption follows colonial and cultural influence patterns

**Source**: [LIS Academy - Global Popularity](https://lis.academy/organising-and-managing-information/library-classification-systems-global-popularity/)

## Alternative Classification Systems

### Dewey Decimal Classification (DDC)
- **Global leader**: Most widely used worldwide
- **Origins**: Published 1876 by Melvil Dewey (U.S.)
- **Biases**: Also exhibits Western bias in coverage of non-Western religions, racial groups, languages, and literatures
- **Usage**: Popular in public and school libraries
- **Limitations**: Product of 19th century America, embedded with belief in "Universal Reason"

**Sources**:
- [LIS Academy - Comparison](https://lis.academy/organising-and-managing-information/library-classification-systems-global-popularity/)
- [John the Librarian - Inherent Bias](https://johnthelibrarian.com/2017/12/13/inherent-bias-in-classification-systems/)

### Universal Decimal Classification (UDC)
- **Origins**: Developed by Paul Otlet and Henri La Fontaine (late 19th century)
- **Design**: Expansion of DDC with more precise/adaptable approach
- **Structure**: Analytic-synthetic approach with complex notation
- **Usage**: Strength in continental Europe
- **Limitations**: More difficult for shelf arrangement, still reflects Western epistemology

**Source**: [LIS Academy - Comparison](https://lis.academy/organising-and-managing-information/library-classification-systems-global-popularity/)

### Chinese Library Classification (CLC)
- **Regional dominance**: Used in over 90% of Chinese libraries and information institutions
- **Origins**: Compiled by 36 institutions starting 1971; 1st edition 1975
- **Influence**: Influenced by DDC principles but adapted for Chinese context
- **Marked Chinese characteristics**: Cultural adaptation
- **Limitation**: DDC itself "unable to be popularized in China"

**Source**: [IFLA - Contemporary Classification Systems in China](https://origin-archive.ifla.org/IV/ifla62/62-qiyz.htm)

### Indigenous Alternatives
- **Brian Deer Classification** (1974): Created by Brian Deer, Kahnawake librarian
- **X̱wi7x̱wa Library** (UBC): Uses adapted Brian Deer system
- **Goal**: Counter Western colonial bias, reflect Indigenous perspectives
- **Example**: Avoids placing Native materials in "History" section

**Source**: [Yes Magazine - Indigenous Library Approach](https://www.yesmagazine.org/social-justice/2019/03/22/decolonize-western-bias-indigenous-library-books)

## Key Research Findings on Classification Bias

### Quantitative Bias Measurement
Research has developed methods for quantifying Western bias in library classification systems:

**Findings from analysis of 3 million+ books**:
- Categories related to **religion show greater Western bias** than literature or history
- **Male author overrepresentation** documented
- Both LCC and DDC are "biased and unsystematic in their coverage of non-western religions and racial groups"

**Source**: [PMC - Quantifying Bias in Hierarchical Category Systems](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10898782/)

### Structural Inevitability
All classification systems reflect their cultural origins:

**Key insight**: "Western bias dictates classification to have mutually exclusive categories, a hierarchical structure, and a linear sequence of subcategories within a category (e.g., from abstract to concrete); such principles are often at odds with thinking in non-Western cultures."

**Historical context**: "Modern classification systems are highly nuanced systems designed to encompass all knowledge; however, they retain some of the same features and biases of their forebears."

**Source**: [John the Librarian - Inherent Bias](https://johnthelibrarian.com/2017/12/13/inherent-bias-in-classification-systems/)

### Reform Challenges

**Practical barriers**:
- Updating schedules "generally assumed to be impractical due to massive workload"
- Systems are "hierarchical, complex, historically biased and slow to change"
- "Discipline-based classes of LCC have been entrenched in the average library user's mind"

**Critical reality**: "Currently there are no other workable subject or classification systems that have the scope and breadth of the Library of Congress systems, and no other systems address the problem of bias adequately."

**Source**: [Stonehill College Library Newsletter](https://www.stonehill.edu/library/library-newsletter/news/the-library-of-congress-outdated-biased-classifications/)

## Implications for SHELF

### Why LCC Despite Biases?

1. **No bias-free alternative exists**: All major systems (DDC, UDC, CLC) exhibit cultural biases
2. **Scope and breadth**: LCC remains most comprehensive system for organizing diverse knowledge
3. **Research library standard**: Dominant in major research institutions globally
4. **Synthetic generation advantage**: SHELF can actively counter structural biases

### How SHELF Mitigates LCC Biases

1. **Balanced LCC distribution**: Near-uniform distribution across all 21 classes (4.6-4.9% each)
2. **Global geographic coverage**: 44 regions spanning all continents
3. **Diverse topic coverage**: 112 topics across all LCC domains
4. **Cross-product diversity**: Documents that challenge typical correlations (e.g., Maps about Philosophy, Jokes about Law)

### Acknowledged Limitations

1. **Inherited classification structure**: SHELF uses LCC as organizational framework
2. **Western epistemology**: Hierarchical, mutually exclusive categories
3. **Terminology**: Some LCC subject headings contain outdated or biased language
4. **Focus on major classes**: SHELF doesn't address subclass-level granularity issues

## Conclusion

LCC exhibits well-documented Western, American, and Christian biases stemming from its historical development. However:

1. **All classification systems are culturally situated**: DDC, UDC, CLC all reflect their origins
2. **LCC remains most comprehensive**: Despite biases, it covers broader scope than alternatives
3. **SHELF's synthetic approach counters structural biases**: Balanced generation across all dimensions
4. **No superior alternative exists**: As Library of Congress acknowledges, "no other workable subject or classification systems" have comparable scope

The claim to "universal" coverage should be understood as:
- **Comprehensive within Western bibliographic tradition** (accurate)
- **Balanced across all LCC classes and forms** (demonstrated)
- **Global geographic and topical coverage** (documented)
- **NOT culturally neutral or bias-free** (acknowledged limitation)
