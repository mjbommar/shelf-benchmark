## Abstract (250 words)

Text embedding benchmarks face two fundamental challenges: domain specificity that limits generalizability (FinMTEB for finance, ChemTEB for chemistry, LegalBench for law), and data contamination from web-scraped corpora that may overlap with model pretraining data. We introduce SHELF (Synthetic Harness for Evaluating LLM Fitness), a contamination-resistant benchmark of 42,616 documents synthetically generated by nine frontier language models (GPT-5.1/5.2, Gemini 2.5 Flash/Pro, Gemini 3 Pro, Claude 4.5 Haiku/Sonnet/Opus) using the Library of Congress Classification system—the world's most comprehensive taxonomy for organizing human knowledge. SHELF achieves controlled factorial diversity across 21 subject classes, 133 document forms, 112 topics, 44 geographic regions, 25 audience types, and 8 writing registers, producing cross-dimensional combinations (e.g., "jokes about military science," "maps about philosophy") that stress-test genuine semantic understanding beyond surface-level correlations found in natural corpora.

We evaluate 23 models spanning sparse baselines (TF-IDF, BM25) and state-of-the-art neural embeddings (BGE, E5, GTE, Instructor, GTR-T5) ranging from 2M to 335M parameters across four task types: classification, retrieval, clustering, and pair matching (358 total evaluations). Our findings challenge prevailing assumptions about neural embedding superiority: sparse TF-IDF+SVD achieves the highest aggregate SHELF score (0.679), outperforming the best neural model (BGE-large-en, 0.513) by 32%. Critically, all neural models underperform the BM25 retrieval baseline on average task performance. We observe dramatic task-specific variance—models achieving 0.88 macro-F1 on subject classification score only 0.14 NDCG@10 on genre retrieval—revealing that current embeddings optimize for semantic similarity rather than the multi-faceted document organization required by information professionals. We release SHELF as an open benchmark with efficiency-adjusted rankings and Pareto frontier analysis.

## Extended Abstract (for workshops/longer formats)

### Problem Statement

Existing text embedding benchmarks suffer from critical limitations:

1. **Domain Specificity**: Benchmarks like FinMTEB (finance), ChemTEB (chemistry), and LegalBench (law) evaluate narrow domains, failing to assess general document understanding capabilities.

2. **Data Contamination**: Web-scraped evaluation corpora (Wikipedia, Common Crawl derivatives) likely overlap with model pretraining data, inflating reported performance.

3. **Correlation Exploitation**: Natural corpora exhibit strong genre-subject correlations (legal briefs about law, medical papers about medicine), allowing models to exploit surface patterns rather than demonstrating genuine understanding.

4. **Single-Task Focus**: Most benchmarks evaluate either classification OR retrieval OR clustering, preventing holistic assessment of embedding quality.

### Our Contribution: SHELF

SHELF (Synthetic Harness for Evaluating LLM Fitness) addresses these limitations through:

**1. Contamination Resistance**
Documents are synthetically generated by frontier LLMs, ensuring zero overlap with any model's pretraining data.

**2. Universal Coverage via Library of Congress Taxonomies**
The LC Classification system was designed to organize *all human knowledge*—from agriculture to zoology. SHELF covers:
- 21 LCC subject classes (uniform 4.6-4.9% distribution)
- 133 LCGFT document forms (lectures, jokes, maps, prayers, etc.)
- 112 LCSH topics
- 44 geographic regions
- 25 audience types
- 8 writing registers

**3. Factorial Independence**
Unlike natural corpora where genre and subject correlate strongly, SHELF achieves independence across dimensions. Every LCC class appears with every genre category, producing combinations like:
- Jokes about Philosophy
- Maps about Law
- Prayers about Technology
- Legal briefs about Music

This cross-product diversity is *more comprehensive than real-world corpora*.

**4. Multi-Task Evaluation**
The same 42,616 documents support:
- **Classification**: LCC (21-way), Genre (14-way), Register (8-way)
- **Retrieval**: Subject-based, Form-based, Category-based
- **Clustering**: K-means, HDBSCAN, Agglomerative (12 tasks)
- **Pair Classification**: Same-subject, same-form, topic-overlap (6 tasks)

### Key Findings

| Finding | Implication |
|---------|-------------|
| TF-IDF+SVD (0.679) > BGE-large (0.513) | Sparse methods still competitive on diverse content |
| All neural models < BM25 baseline | Neural embeddings may overfit to benchmark distributions |
| 0.88 F1 on LCC vs 0.14 NDCG on form retrieval | Embeddings optimize for semantic similarity, not document organization |
| Small models (BGE-small) achieve 97% of large model performance | Diminishing returns from scale on this task |
| Geographic clustering near-random (V-measure 0.02) | Current embeddings don't capture geographic content well |

### Implications

1. **For Practitioners**: Don't assume neural embeddings are universally superior. Evaluate on your actual task distribution.

2. **For Researchers**: There is significant room for improvement in document understanding beyond semantic similarity.

3. **For Benchmark Design**: Synthetic benchmarks with controlled diversity can reveal model limitations that natural benchmarks obscure.
